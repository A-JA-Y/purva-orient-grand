Most rent vs buy house guides for Bangalore are written for the tech corridors, with a ₹1 crore flat and a ₹40,000 rent. The city centre is a different sum. Homes are larger, prices are several crore, and very few new ones get built.

This page works it through for a 3 BHK at Purva Orient Grand on Lalbagh Road, listed from ₹6.00 crore\*. It is arithmetic, not advice.

## Rent and Price in Central Bangalore

**Rent.** A 3 BHK for rent in central Bangalore, of about 2,000 sq. ft. in a good building, asks roughly ₹1.4 lakh to ₹1.75 lakh a month around Richmond Road and Shanthi Nagar. Older flats in Richmond Town start near ₹90,000. This page uses ₹1.5 lakh.

**Price.** The 3 BHK here is 1,902 to 2,262 sq. ft. of [super built-up area](/configurations), from ₹6.00 crore\*. That is about ₹28,000 to ₹30,000 per sq. ft.

**Yield.** A year’s rent at ₹1.5 lakh a month is 3% of ₹6 crore. Bengaluru as a whole yields about 4.6%, the highest of India’s big cities, so the centre is costlier to own relative to rent than the suburbs.

**Trend.** Luxury values in central Bengaluru rose about 13% to 14% in the year to mid-2026. Rents across the city are rising too, by about 2.5% in a single quarter of 2026.

## Rent vs EMI: A Worked Example

Take a 3 BHK at ₹6.00 crore\*.

**Buying**

- Price: ₹6.00 crore
- GST at 5%: ₹30 lakh
- Stamp duty, registration and cess at 7.6%: about ₹45.6 lakh
- Down payment: ₹1.5 crore, since banks lend at most 75% on loans above ₹75 lakh
- Your own money in total: about ₹2.26 crore
- Loan: ₹4.5 crore
- EMI at 8% for 20 years: about ₹3.76 lakh a month
- Upkeep and charges a tenant wouldn’t pay: say ₹15,000 a month

**Renting**

- A similar 3 BHK nearby: about ₹1.5 lakh a month, rising about 5% a year

## Stamp Duty and Registration Charges in Bangalore

Karnataka’s costs changed in 2025, and they are the same for every buyer.

- **7.6% in all.** That is 5% stamp duty, 2% registration and 0.6% cess and surcharge. The registration fee doubled from 1% on 31 August 2025.
- **No concession for women.** Unlike Maharashtra or Uttar Pradesh, the rate doesn’t change with the buyer.
- **GST depends on timing.** An under-construction home pays 5%. A home bought after its completion certificate pays none. On ₹6 crore that is ₹30 lakh, so ask where the tower stands and what is still available.

## When Does Buying Break Even?

Owning costs more each month, so it wins only if the home gains value. Suppose the tenant invests the ₹2.26 crore and the monthly gap. How fast must the home appreciate for the owner to end up level?

| If you stay | Tenant’s money earns 7% | Earns 9% | Earns 11% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 years | 6.3% a year | 7.5% a year | 8.8% a year |
| 15 years | 5.8% a year | 7.2% a year | 8.7% a year |
| 20 years | 5.4% a year | 7.1% a year | 8.9% a year |

*Illustration only. It uses the figures above, rent rising 5% a year and a tenant who invests every rupee of the difference. Selling costs and tax on gains are left out.*

If the tenant’s money earns 9%, the home has to gain about 7.5% a year over ten years. That means ₹6 crore becoming about ₹12.4 crore.

<PullQuote>
Rent growth matters more in Bengaluru than in most cities. If rents rise 8% a year instead of 5%, the bar falls to 7.1% over ten years and 6.2% over twenty.
</PullQuote>

Rent then catches up with the EMI in about 12 years instead of about 20.

Buying without GST also moves it. With no GST, the ten-year bar is about 6.9%.

Central luxury values have risen faster than these bars lately. One strong year is not a ten-year average, and nobody can promise the next decade.

## Tax on Rent and Home Loans

- **Tenants:** HRA relief is available only in the old regime. Bengaluru joins the 50% list from FY 2026-27, so the salary-based limit is 50%, up from 40%.
- **TDS on rent:** an individual paying more than ₹50,000 a month must deduct 2% TDS from the rent. On ₹1.5 lakh a month that is ₹36,000 a year, paid on the landlord’s account.
- **Owners, old regime:** you can deduct up to ₹2 lakh of interest a year on a self-occupied home. Against ₹35.7 lakh of first-year interest, that barely registers.
- **New regime:** neither HRA nor the interest deduction is available.

Check your own position with a tax adviser.

## Conclusion

On rent vs buy house in Bangalore, the centre is its own case. Rent is about 40% of the EMI, and buying needs about 7.5% a year for a decade to match. The case for buying rests on two things particular to this city: rents that keep climbing, and how few new homes come up near Lalbagh. If you’ll stay ten years and can carry the EMI comfortably, buying is reasonable. If your plans are open, rent in the centre and keep the money working.
