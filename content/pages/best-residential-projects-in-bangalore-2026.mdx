## How we picked the best residential projects in Bangalore

- A live Karnataka RERA registration you can find yourself, with the completion date read off the portal, not the brochure.
- A developer with occupied projects in this city. National brand size counts for less than local handovers.
- Land and open ground per home. The clubhouse list is the easiest thing to inflate; the acreage is not.
- A location with a reason to hold value: the airport corridor, an IT hub, the Metro, or, in the centre, land that simply is not being made any more.
- A price confirmed in November 2026. Where a portal and the developer disagreed, the developer won.

Three of the five are in North Bangalore because that is where the land parcels large enough for a township or a villa precinct still exist. One is in Whitefield and one is in the centre, and those two are on the list precisely because new supply in both places is rare.

## Which zone should you buy in?

**Central Bangalore** (Lalbagh, Richmond Town, the CBD) gives you established streets, no commute and almost no new supply. For property investment in Bangalore’s centre you are paying for permanence rather than growth, and the land share of the price is what you are really buying. Our [CBD investment guide](/blogs/why-cbd-bangalore-property-appreciates) sets out the numbers, and our guide to [luxury apartments in central Bangalore](/blogs/luxury-apartments-central-bangalore) shows how Purva Orient Grand stacks up against the rest of the CBD on land, density and delivery.

**North Bangalore** is where the land is. Residential projects in North Bangalore now run from ₹67 lakh studios at Thanisandra to ₹14 crore-plus villas at Tharahunise, driven by the airport, the aerospace and hardware parks and the Blue Line Metro. The catch is that daily infrastructure in the outer belt is still catching up, so you are buying ahead of it.

**East Bangalore** (Whitefield and the IT belt) has the deepest end-user and rental demand because the offices are already there. Expect higher density and a higher price per sq ft for the same budget, and almost no villa land, which is why a 20-acre row villa project on Soukya Road made the cut. **South Bangalore** has launches this year too, but none that beat the five above on our checks.

If you are still deciding whether the city itself suits you, our guide to [whether Bangalore is good to live or invest in](/is-bangalore-good-to-live-or-invest-in) compares the core and the corridors on prices, rents and risks.

## Villa or apartment?

The best villa projects in Bangalore are in the north and on the eastern fringe, because that is where 20-plus acre parcels still exist. A villa buys you land, a private garden and no shared walls, and you pay for it at purchase and in upkeep. An apartment in a well-run township gives you a lower ticket, easier resale and amenities you will actually use.

The rule of thumb we use with clients in the suburbs is simple: below ₹5 crore, buy the best apartment you can in the zone you work in; above ₹10 crore, buy land. The centre needs a footnote. There is no villa land in central Bangalore, and at Purva Orient Grand land is about half the project cost, so a ₹9 crore apartment beside Lalbagh is closer to a land purchase than the number suggests.

## Before you book: six things to check

1. **The RERA number and what it covers.** Confirm it on rera.karnataka.gov.in. Township registrations usually cover one cluster or phase, and the registered name can differ from the marketing name.
2. **Carpet area, not super built-up.** Compare on the RERA carpet figure; the gap is widest on villas. Our [floor plans](/floor-plans) page lists both for every grade.
3. **The all-inclusive cost.** GST, stamp duty, registration, floor rise, corpus, deposits and clubhouse fees add a large amount to the base price. Get the working in writing.
4. **The developer’s record in Bangalore specifically.** Occupied projects here matter more than brand size; the [About Puravankara](/about-puravankara) page is the kind of record to ask every developer for.
5. **The possession date in the agreement**, not the sales deck. The RERA-filed date is the one you can enforce; ask about the OC timeline as well.
6. **The commute at 6 pm on a weekday.** No map replaces this, even in the CBD.
