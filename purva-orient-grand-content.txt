SECTION 3 — PROJECT OVERVIEW (Homepage body copy)
================================================================================

Central Bengaluru does not make new land. Everything built here since 2010 has
come from redeveloping something small — which is why projects in the CBD are
boutique by necessity, not by marketing choice.

Purva Orient Grand is 1.15 acres on Lal Bagh Main Road at Sudhama Nagar. One
tower, two basements plus ground plus twenty-four floors, and ninety-seven homes
in total. That is the whole project. It belongs to Puravankara's WorldHome
Collection, the group's ultra-luxury line.

Two design decisions define it.

The first is that no residence shares a common wall with another. In a
single-tower project that is expensive to achieve — it costs you saleable area —
and it is the thing residents notice every day. Every home gets private decks and
independent envelope on multiple sides.

The second is the roof. The twenty-fourth and twenty-fifth levels are given over
entirely to The Rose & Cypress, a Victorian-inspired rooftop club designed by the
Singapore-based architect Andy Fisher. It carries an infinity pool at the
twenty-fifth level, a whisky lounge called The Vault, a spa, a salon, a fitness
studio, a larder room, a firepit lounge and a rooftop dining terrace. On a
ninety-seven-home project, that is roughly one club for every hundred residents.

Then there is what the site has that money cannot buy elsewhere: Lalbagh
Botanical Garden, two hundred and forty acres of protected land, five minutes
away. It is not a park that a developer might one day acquire. It is a protected
botanical garden. The green in front of these homes is permanent in a way that
almost nothing else in Bengaluru is.

The homes come in four grades — 3 BHK Super Premium, 4 BHK Elegance, 4 BHK
Luxury in the corner positions, and 4 BHK Grandeur, also corner, at 3,095 to
3,360 sq ft. Each is fitted with BluNex home automation, imported marble in the
social areas and laminated wood in the bedrooms.

The design team is unusually credited: Urban Frame on concept architecture, SDG
Mumbai on the façade, Master Plan Chennai on landscape, Andy Fisher of Singapore
on the rooftop club.

The project is registered with Karnataka RERA with a filed completion date of
30 June 2027, and is in its final stages of construction.

SECTION 6 — MASTER PLAN
================================================================================

A single tower on 1.15 acres, organised vertically because the site gives no
other option — and the vertical organisation is what makes the project work.

Two basement levels carry the parking, which keeps the entire ground plane
available for landscape. The ground level holds the arrival court and sculpture
court, a koi pond, a healing garden, a yoga deck, a zumba deck and a seating
court.

The podium and terrace level carries the active family programme: a children's
activity trail, Tetris block seating, an outdoor table tennis zone, a paw park
for pets, a basketball post and a mini golf putting green.

Above that, twenty-four residential floors. Ground and first levels carry four
homes each; the mix varies across floors two to twenty-four.

The twenty-fourth and twenty-fifth levels are given over entirely to The Rose &
Cypress club and the infinity pool, with a moon deck and private terrace above
the pool deck. Sanctioned floor area ratio is 4.28 — a CBD figure, and the reason
a boutique project like this is viable on so small a parcel.

SECTION 8 — SPECIFICATIONS
================================================================================

STRUCTURE
MIVAN aluminium formwork RCC superstructure, seismic compliant. Solid concrete
block masonry — 200 mm external walls and 100 mm internal.

MIVAN is worth understanding as a buyer. It produces a monolithic concrete
structure with far tighter dimensional tolerances than conventional block-and-
plaster construction, which means flatter walls, fewer cracks over time and
better acoustic performance between spaces. It is more expensive to build and is
one of the reasons this project is priced where it is.

FLOORING
Imported marble in living, dining, foyer and passages. Laminated wooden flooring
in the master bedroom and bedrooms. Anti-skid ceramic tiles in wet areas.
Large-format vitrified tiles in the common areas.

KITCHEN

chimney, hob, water purifier, dishwasher and utility points.

DOORS

SECTION 10 — WHY CENTRAL BENGALURU
================================================================================

The case for the CBD is not about amenities. Every Rs 8 crore project in
Bengaluru has an infinity pool. The case is about land.

Look at this project's own RERA filing. Total project cost is approximately Rs
228 crore, of which land accounts for Rs 119.4 crore — fifty-two per cent. On a
suburban project that ratio is typically twenty to thirty per cent. When land is
more than half the cost of a building, it tells you something no brochure can:
that the ground itself is the scarce input, and that scarcity does not
depreciate.

What follows from it:

New supply in Central Bengaluru arrives only through redevelopment of small
parcels. That is why boutique projects of ten to sixty units dominate the
segment, and why ninety-seven homes on 1.15 acres is a normal CBD format rather
than an unusually small one.

Price tiering in the CBD runs roughly like this. Ultra-premium — Lavelle Road,
UB City — transacts at Rs 35,000 to Rs 55,000 per sq ft. Premium Richmond Town
and Sadashivanagar at Rs 20,000 to Rs 28,000. Purva Orient Grand at Rs 27,900 to
Rs 30,100 sits at the top of premium, just under the ultra-premium core.

The Lalbagh adjacency is the irreplaceable part. A protected two-hundred-and-
forty-acre botanical garden cannot be acquired, subdivided or built over. The
low-rise green buffer in front of these homes is permanent, and permanence is
precisely what you are buying in the CBD.

The comparable set is Prestige Kenilworth Residences at Richmond Town, Sobha
Indraprastha, Embassy Habitat at Vasanth Nagar and Spectra Cypress at Richmond
Town — all boutique, all redevelopment, all priced on the same scarcity logic.

The buyer base here is not the IT corridor buyer. It is corporate leadership,
professionals with practices in the CBD, old-Bangalore families trading up within
their own neighbourhood, and NRIs who want an address that will still read
correctly in twenty years.

cities. Purva Land handles plotted development, Purva Streaks handles interiors,
and Starworth Infrastructure is the in-house EPC arm with over 21 million sq ft
built. The company is ISO 9001 certified.

Purva Orient Grand belongs to the WorldHome Collection, Puravankara's ultra-
luxury line. Other current Bengaluru projects include Purva Zenium, Purva
Blubelle, Purva Heritage and Purva Silversky.
